**Sarvkrit lives in the menu bar and fixes the things macOS does differently than you'd expect.** Everything ships switched off; turn on only what you want.

Twenty features now. The new one records your screen — and then edits the recording for you.

## Screen Recording

**⌃⇧R** opens a bar: what to record, which camera, which microphone, how long a countdown. The camera preview is live before you press Record, because discovering that the camera was off — or pointed at the ceiling — after a ten-minute take is the most expensive thing this feature could do to you.

**⌃⇧E** records an area. The rectangle is already on screen at the size you used last time, with handles to adjust it, rather than an empty screen to drag on. A recording is aimed once and then lived with; it should open as something you adjust. **⌃⇧U** pauses and resumes, and **⌃⇧M** marks the moment you're in so you can find it again in the editor.

While recording, a small pill shows the elapsed time, pause, stop and discard. It fades back a few seconds in so it stops competing with whatever you're demonstrating, and wakes when you move the pointer near it. It never disappears — stopping should not involve a hunt.

### It edits itself

![Sarvkrit's editing timeline: a Video row showing a 32-second clip with 3.4 seconds trimmed, a Camera row with full-frame and hidden stretches, and a Zoom row holding five automatic zooms at 2.5x, 1.7x, 2.5x, 2.5x and 1.4x, three of them following the cursor](https://github.com/sadana-psylief/Sarvkrit/raw/v1.2.0/docs/images/timeline.png)

Stop a recording and the editor opens with the work already done: the zooms found, and the dead seconds before the camera and microphone came up trimmed off the front — with a banner saying how much and a button to put it back.

**The zooms are the point.** Sarvkrit watches where you clicked and where the pointer actually rested, and picks how close to go from how spread out the activity was: a click on one button goes closer than a slow sweep across a region. Where the pointer is genuinely travelling — a fling across the screen to reach something — it's ignored, because where you moved the mouse from is not what you're showing. Two zooms close together are one continuous move from one level to the next, rather than zooming out to nothing in between and back in again.

Everything it decided is yours to change. Split clips, reorder them, set speed per clip, add text, pictures, zooms, click effects and pointer highlights by hand. Blur or pixelate a region and drag it or resize it by a corner. A camera bubble you can move, reshape and hide for a stretch. Captions, transcribed on your Mac.

### Exporting

Sharpest keeps every pixel that was recorded. Web is 1080p and plays everywhere; Small is 720p for sending in a message; For an editor is ProRes at full size. Or set the resolution, format and frame rate yourself — 4K is offered even where the recording cannot fill it, and it says so rather than quietly capping you.

**A recording is a file you can reopen.** It saves as a `.sarvrec` you can double-click in the Finder weeks later, change and export again — the edits are a document, not something baked into a video.

## Screenshots

Everything from 1.1 — area, window, fullscreen, scrolling capture, text recognition, the annotation editor, Pin to Screen — plus a round of fixes that came out of going through it properly:

- **Deleted captures go to the Trash.** "Delete All Captures" used to wipe everything on a single click, with no confirmation and nothing to recover. So did the retention sweep, which runs by itself when the app starts and clears anything past the window you chose. Both now ask, or are recoverable, or both.
- **An edited capture stays editable.** Annotating a screenshot and reopening it from history used to hand you the annotations as pixels — the arrow could no longer be moved or deleted. It comes back as a document now.
- The window picker lists the size the file will actually be, rather than half of it on a Retina display.
- Window capture and text recognition can be driven from a script, like area capture already could.

## Install

With Homebrew:

```
brew install --cask sadana-psylief/tap/sarvkrit
```

Homebrew removes the quarantine attribute itself, so the Gatekeeper detour below does not apply. Already have a copy in `/Applications` that Homebrew did not put there? Add `--force`.

Or by hand:

1. Download `Sarvkrit.dmg` below and drag **Sarvkrit** to your Applications folder. Put it there rather than leaving it in Downloads — macOS keys permissions to where the app lives.
2. **First launch: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.** Only the first time.

   Double-click Sarvkrit, click **Done** on the warning, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to **Security**, and click **Open Anyway** next to Sarvkrit. Authenticate, open Sarvkrit again and choose **Open**. From then on it opens normally.

   That second step is not optional, and it is not a warning you can ignore: this build is signed but **not notarized**, because notarization needs a paid Apple Developer account. Double-clicking alone will get you "Apple could not verify Sarvkrit is free of malware" and nothing else.

   Not *right-click → Open*: macOS 15 Sequoia removed that override, so on 15 and later it shows the same refusal with no Open button. Open Anyway works on 14 too, so it is the only route given here. If you would rather use a terminal, `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Sarvkrit.app` does the same thing.

Or skip the whole detour with one command:

```
curl -fsSL https://sarvkrit.com/install | sh
```

That works because macOS quarantines what a *browser* downloaded and curl sets no such attribute, so Gatekeeper's first-launch check never runs. Which is why the script does the equivalent check itself — signature intact, signed by this team — and installs nothing if either fails.

## Upgrading from 1.1.x

`brew upgrade --cask sarvkrit`, or drag the new copy over the old one. Your settings, clipboard history, snippets, shelf and capture history are all kept.

Screen Recording is off, like every other feature. Turning it on asks for Screen Recording permission, and for the camera and microphone only if you choose to use them — a recorder that films you by default is a recorder that has filmed you without your deciding to be filmed.

## Requirements

- **macOS 14.4 or later.** Core Audio process taps are the only public way to do per-app volume and they don't exist before 14.4; everything else here would run on 14.0.
- **Universal** — Apple Silicon and Intel (`x86_64 arm64`).

## What's in it

Twenty independent toggles across seven groups:

- **Keyboard** — Finder Cut & Paste (⌘X then ⌘V), Text Snippets
- **Clipboard** — Clipboard History (⌘⇧C)
- **Windows** — Window Management (⌃⌥ + arrows), Quit on Close
- **Capture** — Screenshots (⌃⇧A), Screen Recording (⌃⇧R), Pin to Screen (⌃⇧P)
- **Files** — File Rules, Trash Cleanup, App Sweep, Shelf (⌃⌥S)
- **Sound** — Audio Devices (⌃⌥O), Mute Microphone (⌃⌥M), Music Blocker, Volume Mixer, Privacy Guard
- **System** — Displays, Keep Awake, System Monitor

## Verifying the download

```
shasum -a 256 Sarvkrit.dmg
@SHA@
```

The app inside is signed and passes `codesign --verify --strict`.
